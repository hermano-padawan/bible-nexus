> “Don’t therefore be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Each day’s own evil is sufficient.” — Matthew 6:34, World English Bible

Tomorrow can feel very large when you are already tired today. Your mind runs ahead, preparing conversations that have not happened and solving problems that have not arrived. You imagine every possible outcome because uncertainty feels unsafe.

Jesus brings us back to today. He does not say tomorrow is unimportant. He says we are not meant to carry tomorrow’s trouble before tomorrow comes.

## Grace meets you in the day you are living

In Matthew 6, Jesus points to birds and wildflowers. Birds still gather food, and flowers still grow where God has planted them. His words are not an invitation to ignore rent, appointments, deadlines, or responsibilities. They are an invitation to remember that life is more than our attempt to control it.

Worry promises preparation, but often it only spends today’s strength on an imagined future. Careful planning can produce a useful next step. Anxiety keeps walking around the same question after no new answer is available.

God gives grace in real time. He met Israel with manna for each day in the wilderness. The people could not store it as a private guarantee against every future need. They learned to receive again in the morning.

You may want enough certainty for the next year. God may be giving enough light for the next hour. That gift can feel small, but it is still grace.

## Waiting is not doing nothing

Isaiah says that those who wait for the Lord will renew their strength. Biblical waiting is not empty passivity. It is continuing to live faithfully while the answer remains outside your control.

You can wait for test results and still eat, rest, pray, and let someone sit with you. You can wait for work and still prepare an application. You can wait for direction and still keep the commitments already in front of you. You can wait for reconciliation without forcing another person to respond before they are ready.

Waiting with God means refusing to make uncertainty the center of every moment. There is still a person beside you to love. There is still beauty to notice. There may be useful work for your hands today, even though the larger question has no answer.

The waiting season is part of your life, but it does not have to consume your whole life.

## Leave tomorrow in God’s care

Trust does not mean believing that tomorrow will contain no pain. Jesus never promised His followers an easy road. Trust means tomorrow will not arrive before God does.

The Lord already knows the place you cannot see. He will not be surprised by the call, the bill, the conversation, or the change. You will meet that day with whatever it actually contains, not with all the versions fear invents tonight.

This does not give us control. It gives us Someone to trust when control is impossible.

Open your hands for a moment. Name one thing about tomorrow that you have been holding too tightly. Tell God why it frightens you. Then say, slowly, “This matters to me, and I place it in Your care.” You may need to say it again later. Returning worries to God is often a repeated act, not a single perfect moment.

## Receive today without rushing through it

Waiting can make the present feel like a hallway rather than a room. You move through it impatiently because the “real life” you want has not begun. Yet God is present here too.

Notice something ordinary: warm water, bread, a familiar voice, sunlight on the floor, the ability to take one more breath. Gratitude does not deny what is missing. It keeps what is missing from erasing what has been given.

Ask what love requires today. Perhaps it is listening to your child, answering one email, visiting someone, or treating your own tired body with patience. Small faithfulness is not a distraction from God’s plan. Very often, it is where His plan is lived.

## When waiting has exhausted you

Sometimes waiting is more than uncomfortable. It can disturb sleep, appetite, concentration, and hope. Do not turn this devotional into another demand to be strong.

Tell someone you trust. Ask for practical help. If anxiety or sadness is making everyday life difficult, speaking with a doctor, counselor, or other qualified professional can be wise. God’s care is not limited to what happens in private prayer. He often helps us through people who know how to listen and support us.

Rest is also a faithful response. Elijah once reached a point of deep exhaustion, and God first gave him sleep, food, and gentle care. Your body is not an obstacle to your spiritual life. It is part of the life God has given you.

## For today

Take one slow breath. Thank God for one good thing that is here now. Place one worry in His hands. Then do the next kind and faithful thing you know to do.

You do not need tomorrow’s strength yet. Today, ask for today’s bread, today’s wisdom, and today’s peace.

## For remembering today

**God’s grace will meet me in the day I am actually living.**

## Prayer

Father, tomorrow feels heavy, and I keep trying to live it before it comes. Bring me back to this day. Thank You for the grace already here. Show me the next faithful thing to do and help me release what I cannot control. Give me patience while I wait, courage to ask for help, and rest when I am tired. I place tomorrow in Your hands because I know I will not enter it alone. Amen.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does trusting God mean I should not plan?

No. Wise planning considers the facts and prepares responsibly. Trust keeps planning from becoming an attempt to control every outcome. Make the plan you can make, then leave what you cannot guarantee with God.

### What can I do when the same worry returns?

Bring it to God again without condemning yourself. Write down any practical action it requires. If no action is possible today, return your attention to the present task and seek support when the worry becomes overwhelming.

### Is waiting a sign that I missed God’s will?

Not necessarily. Many faithful people in Scripture experienced long waits. Use the season to remain honest, seek wisdom, and do what is already clear rather than assuming every delay is punishment.
