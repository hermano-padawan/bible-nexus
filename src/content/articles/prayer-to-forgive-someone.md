Lord,

You know what happened.

You know the words, choices, betrayal, or neglect that caused harm. You know what I lost and why the memory still hurts.

I will not pretend it was harmless. I bring the truth to You.

## Meet me in the wound

Father, I am angry, sad, confused, or tired. Part of me wants the other person to understand exactly what they have done. Part of me may want them to hurt as I have hurt.

I do not hide those feelings from You. Receive them without allowing them to become my master.

Where I blame myself for another person’s sin, help me put responsibility in the right place. Where I have also done wrong, show me clearly and lead me to confess my part without taking blame that is not mine.

Give me safe people who can listen without rushing me.

## Teach me what forgiveness is

Help me release personal revenge into Your hands. Keep me from feeding fantasies of humiliation or harm.

I understand that forgiveness does not call evil good. It does not erase consequences, cancel justice, or require immediate reconciliation. It does not mean I must trust someone who remains unsafe.

Teach me to desire freedom from hatred while still telling the truth. Let forgiveness be an act of obedience that may need to be repeated as memories return.

I cannot force my feelings to change at once. Work patiently in me.

## Protect me with wise boundaries

If contact is unsafe, give me courage to create distance and seek appropriate help. If laws have been broken or someone is in danger, guide me toward the authorities or professionals who should be involved.

Do not let religious pressure send me back into abuse. Jesus taught forgiveness and also brought evil into the light.

Help me distinguish punishment from protection. A boundary can be necessary without becoming revenge. Give me clear words, support, and a plan.

Where reconciliation may be possible, let it grow from truth, repentance, change, and rebuilt trust—not from pretending nothing happened.

## Release me from the need to control the outcome

I want an apology. I want acknowledgment. I want the story corrected in the minds of everyone who heard only one side.

Those desires may be understandable, but I cannot force another person to repent. Keep my healing from depending entirely on a response I cannot control.

I place their conscience before You. You see fully and judge rightly. Protect me from appointing myself as final judge.

Show me what action belongs to me: speaking truth, documenting what happened, seeking counsel, making a report, or stepping away. Help me leave the final outcome with You.

## Remember the grace You have given me

Father, I also live by mercy. Through Jesus, You have forgiven sins I could not repair by myself.

Let that grace soften me without confusing my story with the other person’s. Remembering my need for forgiveness does not make their harm acceptable. It keeps me from believing I stand above the need for grace.

Where pride has joined my pain, expose it gently. Where compassion can grow without compromising safety, let it grow.

I do not want bitterness to shape the person I become.

## Help me when the memory returns

Some days I believe I have forgiven, and then a place, message, date, or conversation brings everything back.

Keep me from assuming that renewed pain means no forgiveness happened. Wounds can hurt while they heal. Lead me again toward truth and release.

When the memory appears, help me say: “God, You know. I give judgment to You again. Show me the safe and faithful step now.”

If trauma has left deep effects, lead me toward qualified, trauma-informed care. Give me patience with a healing process that does not follow a simple schedule.

## Guard my speech and actions

Keep me from spreading the story merely to damage someone’s reputation. At the same time, do not confuse secrecy with grace. There are people who need accurate information for safety, accountability, counsel, or justice.

Give me wisdom about whom to tell, what to say, and why I am saying it. Let my words be truthful rather than exaggerated.

Stop me before I send the message written only to wound. Give me enough time to choose a response consistent with Christ.

## Restore what harm has taken from me

The injury may have affected confidence, relationships, sleep, work, or the way I see myself.

Remind me that what another person did does not define my worth. Restore the ability to receive healthy love. Help me recognize trustworthy people and enjoy good things without waiting for danger in every moment.

Where material repair is possible, provide it. Where nothing can return exactly as it was, create a future that is not controlled by the past.

Do not waste my pain, but do not ask me to be grateful for evil. Bring good by Your power without renaming what happened.

## Give me freedom one step at a time

Today I release the right to personal revenge. Tomorrow I may need to release it again.

I ask You to act with justice and mercy according to Your perfect knowledge. I ask You to protect others from harm. I ask You to lead the person who hurt me toward real repentance.

Most of all, keep their action from owning the rest of my life.

Fill the space bitterness occupied with truth, wise love, meaningful work, safe relationships, and Your presence.

Jesus, You know betrayal, false accusation, violence, and wounds. Stay near to me. Teach me forgiveness without denial, courage without cruelty, and hope without false promises.

I place this long and difficult work in Your hands.

Amen.

## Frequently asked questions

### Must I reconcile immediately after forgiving?

No. Forgiveness and reconciliation are related but distinct. Reconciliation requires truth, safety, and rebuilt trust, and it may not be possible in every situation.

### Can I forgive and still report what happened?

Yes. Forgiveness does not remove accountability or the need to protect people. Reporting abuse or crime can be a truthful and responsible action.

### Why does the anger return?

Emotional healing often takes time. Returning anger does not automatically mean your earlier choice was false. Bring it to God, maintain wise boundaries, and seek skilled support where needed.
